METHODOLOGY

How to read this

Who's included: only NCAA players flagged as likely in their final year of eligibility (age 22+ or their 4th+ observed NCAA season in our data). This is a heuristic, not a roster fact — class year isn't in the source data, so it's inferred and can be wrong at the margins.

Percentiles: same methodology as the ECHL Player Analytics site — position-specific, season-specific, per-game rates, lower-is-better metrics inverted (Puck Loss Rate, DZ Puck Losses, and similar "against" metrics).

Comp matching: each prospect is compared to historical NCAA seniors on their full percentile profile (metrics both players have data for), restricted to the same position. The 10 closest matches become the comp list.

Outcome labels: comp outcomes come from tracking the same player across every league in our dataset (NCAA, NHL, AHL, ECHL, DEL) by name and date of birth. Only NCAA classes with enough runway to see an outcome (2018-19 through 2023-24) are used as comps.

League weighting is tunable: the Comp Score is a weighted average across comps using an editable league-value scale (NHL > AHL ≈ DEL > ECHL). Changing those weights changes the score, not the underlying comps or probabilities.

Read the odds honestly: across this year's flagged senior class, average NHL probability sits in the low single digits — that's the real base rate, not a bug. Most senior comps land in ECHL/AHL territory or no resolved pro outcome at all.

Data source: InStat player-tracking data, 2018-19 through 2025-26.
